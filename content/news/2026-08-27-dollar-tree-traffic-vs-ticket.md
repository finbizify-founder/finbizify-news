### Dollar Tree's sales growth came mostly from customers spending more per visit

Dollar Tree reported second-quarter net sales grew 7.0% to $4.9 billion. Comparable store net sales,
which counts only stores open at least a year, rose 3.7%, made up of a 3.3 percentage point increase
in average ticket and a 0.4 percentage point increase in traffic.

**Concept:** traffic vs. ticket

A store's sales can grow because more customers walk through the door, because each customer spends
more per visit, or some mix of both. Splitting the change into these two pieces shows which one
actually moved. A rising average ticket means existing customers are buying more per trip. Rising
traffic means more people are choosing to shop there in the first place, which is a different kind of
growth.

**Why it matters:** worth asking of any retailer that reports sales growth: is it because more people
are choosing to shop there, or because the same customers are buying more per visit?
