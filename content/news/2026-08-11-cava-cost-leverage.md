### Cava's head office costs grew more slowly than its sales

Cava reported second-quarter revenue of $368.4 million, compared with $280.6 million in the same
quarter a year earlier. General and administrative expenses rose to $39.8 million from $32.1
million, which works out to 10.8% of revenue against 11.4% a year earlier, and the company said the
decline as a share of revenue came primarily from higher sales spreading those costs further, the
timing of a conference held in the prior-year quarter, and the timing of performance-based incentive
compensation.

**Concept:** fixed vs. variable cost

Some costs move with how much a company sells. Serve twice as many bowls and you buy roughly twice
as much chicken. Other costs sit at head office and stay close to flat whether sales rise or fall,
like the finance team, the software, and the insurance. When sales grow quickly, that second group
gets spread across more revenue, so it takes a smaller share of every dollar even though the dollar
amount went up.

**Why it matters:** worth asking of any company growing quickly: are its costs growing at the same
rate as its sales, or more slowly?
